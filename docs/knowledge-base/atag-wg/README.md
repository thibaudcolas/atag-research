# ATAG WG

> The [Authoring Tool Accessibility Guidelines Working Group (ATAG WG)](https://www.w3.org/groups/wg/atag/) will develop guidelines for authoring tools that are accessible to authors with disabilities, and that enable the production of accessible content. Its mission is to produce an update to [ATAG](../atag/README.md), and to maintain ATAG 2.0 and its support materials.

The group does not exist yet. The [proposed charter](https://www.w3.org/2026/11/proposed-atag-wg-charter.html) was sent to the [Advisory Committee](https://www.w3.org/groups/other/ac/) for review in November 2026, with a vote expected by 9 December 2026 (Wendy Reid, [13 November 2026](https://w3c-cg.github.io/atag/minutes/minutes_Nov13_2026)).

## Charter

The [proposed charter](https://www.w3.org/2026/11/proposed-atag-wg-charter.html) is a two-year charter. It will take effect on the date of the Call for Participation, when the charter is approved.

- **Chairs**: Miriam Fukushima and [Wendy Reid](https://www.w3.org/groups/wg/atag/), both W3C Invited Experts. The current [ATAG CG](../atag-cg/README.md) chairs intend to chair the working group, so the CG will need new chairs.
- **Team contacts**: Rémi Bétin (0.08 FTE) and Shawn Lawton Henry (0.01 FTE).
- **Meetings**: Weekly teleconferences, plus the annual [W3C Technical Plenary](https://www.w3.org/events/tpac/), and up to three additional in-person meetings per year.

A Working Group has a wider remit than the [ATAG CG](../atag-cg/README.md): it can publish W3C Recommendations. The CG is not being converted into the working group; the two coexist, and the charter names the CG as an incubator for work the working group may adopt.

### Scope

The group will explore, with the goal of updating ATAG:

- opportunities for updating ATAG 2.0, including references to WCAG 2.2, areas needing clarification, and outdated references and definitions
- gaps between ATAG 2.0 and implementation practice, and guidance that future specifications or supporting documents could address
- changes in authoring tools and platforms since 2015: generative AI in content creation, and API-driven, headless, and web-based platforms
- evolving end user needs across new authoring tools and platforms
- requirements and guidance for accessible AI- or automation-driven authoring tools, covering both the tools and the content they produce

Standards for the accessibility of content are out of scope, as that is [WCAG](../wcag.md)'s domain.

The [CG charter](https://w3c-cg.github.io/atag/ATAG_Charter.html) already expects a review of ATAG 2.0 against WCAG 2.2, and the [ATAG CG](../atag-cg/README.md) is mapping each WCAG 3 provision to a possible ATAG relationship as groundwork for this update.

## Deliverables

| Deliverable                                                                                         | Status                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Next version of ATAG                                                                                | New normative specification. No draft. Expected completion: Working Draft.        |
| [ATAG 2.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/ATAG20/)                                                           | Maintained. Limited to outdated references, definitions, and informative content. |
| [Implementing ATAG 2.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/IMPLEMENTING-ATAG20/)                                 | Maintained, as a Working Group Note.                                              |
| Possibly: use cases and requirements, test suite and implementation report, primers, best practices | Non-normative documents the group may create.                                     |

The next version of ATAG will provide guidelines for designing authoring tools that enable, support, and promote accessible content production, considering the new landscape for authoring tools and the role of the user in producing, maintaining, and consuming content.

### Timeline

- December 2026: first teleconference.
- Q2 2027: editorial revision of ATAG 2.0 and Implementing ATAG 2.0.
- Q3 2027: use cases and requirements for the next version of ATAG.
- Q2 2028: First Public Working Draft of the next version of ATAG.

These dates are targets, not deadlines.

## Participation

The group needs 6 or more active participants, including representatives from key implementors, and editors and test leads for each specification. Chairs, editors, and test leads are expected to contribute half a working day per week; there is no minimum for other participants.

W3C Membership is the usual route into a Working Group. Participants from organizations that are not W3C Members can join as [Invited Experts](https://www.w3.org/invited-experts/), which the charter expects the chairs to offer to established non-Member contributors, including contributors to the CG. That route matters for ATAG, because much of the CG's participation comes from authoring tool developers at organizations that are not W3C Members (Wendy Reid, [13 November 2026](https://w3c-cg.github.io/atag/minutes/minutes_Nov13_2026)).

All participants follow the W3C [Code of Conduct](https://www.w3.org/policies/code-of-conduct/). The [participation instructions](https://www.w3.org/groups/wg/atag/instructions) cover joining and Invited Expert applications.

## Working in public

Technical work will happen in a w3c/atag GitHub repository, named in the [proposed charter](https://www.w3.org/2026/11/proposed-atag-wg-charter.html#public-github), which the public will be able to read and contribute to. Minutes are archived publicly, and the group may use a Member-only mailing list for administrative matters. Meetings themselves are not open to the public.

Deliverables use the [W3C Document license](https://www.w3.org/copyright/document-license/). The group operates under the [W3C Patent Policy](https://www.w3.org/policies/patent-policy/) (version of 15 May 2025), with Royalty-Free licensing.

## Coordination

The charter names these groups for technical coordination:

- **[AG Working Group](https://www.w3.org/groups/wg/ag/)**: keep ATAG guidance consistent with [WCAG 2 and WCAG 3](../wcag.md) developments.
- **[Internationalization Working Group](https://www.w3.org/groups/wg/i18n-core/)**: authoring requirements and best practices for different languages and scripts.
- **[Publishing Maintenance Working Group](https://www.w3.org/groups/wg/pm/)**: publishing use cases and digital publishing requirements.
- **[Media](https://www.w3.org/groups/wg/media/), [Audio](https://www.w3.org/groups/wg/audio/), and [Timed Text](https://www.w3.org/groups/wg/timed-text/) Working Groups**: media production use cases and requirements.
- **[Web & AI Interest Group](https://www.w3.org/groups/ig/webai/)**: AI-related aspects, including use cases and developments to consider.
- **[ATAG CG](../atag-cg/README.md)**: incubation group. Some CG work may be adopted by the working group.
- **Web Developer Experience (WebDX) Community Group**, and potentially other community groups.

All specifications get [horizontal review](https://www.w3.org/guide/documentreview/#how-to-get-horizontal-review) for accessibility, internationalization, privacy, and security, with the [Technical Architecture Group](https://www.w3.org/groups/other/tag/).

Outside W3C, W3C and [Accessibility Standards Canada](https://accessible.canada.ca/) will jointly develop authoring tool accessibility guidelines, per the [ASC–W3C Memorandum of Understanding](https://www.w3.org/2026/10/proposed-asc-w3c-mou.html). The group will also liaise with stakeholder organizations: [ARSO](https://www.arso-oran.org), [CDPF](https://www.cdpf.org.cn), the [European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/), [ETSI](https://www.etsi.org/), [JIS](https://www.jisc.go.jp/), [NIA](https://eng.nia.or.kr), and the [U.S. Access Board](https://www.access-board.gov/).

## Success criteria

As for any W3C Recommendation-track specification, advancing past Candidate Recommendation needs an open test suite for every feature, and at least two independent interoperable implementations. Each specification needs a testing plan from its earliest drafts, sections on security and privacy implications, and a section on accessibility. The group is expected to follow the [Ethical Web Principles](https://www.w3.org/TR/ethical-web-principles/), [Privacy Principles](https://www.w3.org/TR/privacy-principles/), and [Web Platform Design Principles](https://www.w3.org/TR/design-principles/).

!!! note "Testability in authoring tools"

    The success criteria are written for APIs and formats. ATAG success criteria apply to software, so how interoperability and test suites will be demonstrated is one of the open questions for the group. The charter commits to test suites and implementation reports only as non-normative documents the group may create.

## Relationship with the ATAG CG

Both groups stay in place. The CG keeps its lower barrier to entry, and remains where early explorations happen, including work that the working group may later adopt. People can be members of both, of one, or of neither. The CG's [Use Cases and Requirements](../atag-cg/README.md#status) draft is the natural starting point for the charter's use cases and requirements deliverable.

The CG's charter and its review of ATAG 2.0 predate the working group. Where CG outputs become working group inputs, and how the handoff is recorded, is still being worked out (Rémi Bétin, [13 November 2026](https://w3c-cg.github.io/atag/minutes/minutes_Nov13_2026)).
